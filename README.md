## 🌟 What Is Corolla?

Corolla is a generative art project for creating layered flower-like patterns called corollas. Each artwork is built from rings of petals that gather around a central radius. The project is designed as a reproducible catalogue of computational textile compositions. ## 💡 Tips for Better Results

Pay attention to color spread. Corolla tints petals from the spectrum over a dark ground. If the composition feels busy, reduce the number of competing ring styles. If it feels too quiet, add a sharper layer or a more varied color range.

## 🧩 Understanding the Ring Styles

Each corolla is built from rings, and each ring has a personality. Learning the three styles helps you control the mood of a composition.

Smooth rings are the most restful. They create flowing petal outlines that feel rounded, soft, and orderly. They work well as a base layer or as a quiet outer edge that frames a busier center.

Spiky rings bring contrast and movement. The pointed outlines create a sharper silhouette and make the pattern feel more energetic. A single spiky ring can transform a calm composition into something lively.

Dashed rings add rhythm and texture. The broken outline suggests stitching, dashes of thread, or a printed pattern with deliberate gaps. Dashed rings are useful when a composition feels too solid and needs a lighter touch.

Combining all three styles produces the layered corolla look that gives the project its name. The rings accumulate around a radius, building outward like petals opening from a center. Each added layer changes the balance of the whole composition.

## 🎨 Working With Color

Corolla tints its petals from the spectrum over a dark ground. The dark background is important because it makes the colors glow and gives the pattern a textile-like depth. Spectrum tinting means the petals are not limited to one flat color; they can move through a range of hues.

This approach is what makes the compositions feel like dyed fabric or colored thread. Warm and cool tones can sit near each other, creating contrast and flow. Because the colors are layered with the rings, the color story develops as the composition grows.

When you explore color, try comparing a version with a narrow range of hues against a version with a wider spread. Narrow ranges feel cohesive and calm. Wider ranges feel energetic and decorative. Neither is better; they simply serve different design needs.

## 🖱️ A Simple Workflow to Follow

If you are new to generative art, a gentle workflow helps. Begin with a single ring and get familiar with how it looks. Then add a second ring in a different style and watch how the two layers interact.

Next, introduce color variation. Look at how the spectrum tint changes the feeling of the petals. Then try a new seed and compare the result with your earlier composition. This comparison is where the creative value appears: you start to notice which combinations feel right for your goals.

When something stands out, pause and record it. A short note with the seed and a few words about the color and ring combination is enough. Later, those notes become the starting point for a new series of textile-inspired studies.
